# "All You Zombies" — Game Script (English · Prose Edition)

> An original fan-adaptation visual novel, dramatizing the plot structure of
> Robert A. Heinlein's short story of the same name.
> Reading notes: plain text = scene & dialogue; *italics* = the Bartender's (player's)
> inner monologue; [CHOICE] marks player decisions.
> This is an original dramatic adaptation — all dialogue and narration are newly written,
> not reproduced from the source text.
> Revision note: the central reveal (that everyone is the same person) is held until the
> "Revelation" scene near the end of Act 3. Until then, the Bartender (player) feels only
> an inexplicable déjà vu and a dull ache in the chest.

## Prologue: Before Closing Time

The rain has been falling since evening, and it still hasn't decided to stop. Neon light bleeds through the downpour and lays wet streaks of red across the bar. The clock on the wall reads 2:40 in the morning.

*Another night like this one. I've been polishing the same glass for twenty minutes. It's been clean for nineteen of them. I just need something to do with my hands.*

The place is called Pop's. Cheap name, dim lights, strong liquor. The people who come here mostly aren't here for the drink.

*They're here for someone willing to listen. And I happen to be the best listener there is. …Strange, though. I keep feeling like I've heard this story before. A long, long time ago. But how could I have? Tonight is the first time I've ever seen him.*

The door opens and the bell gives one dull ring, reluctant. A young man comes in. Mid-twenties, maybe. Thin, in a men's shirt gone pale with age and a jacket over it. Hair cropped short. He stands in the doorway a moment, as if checking whether anyone else is here. Then he walks to the bar and sits down.

He doesn't speak right away. He just sits there, staring at a scratch in the counter, as if the answer to something were hidden in it.

*I look up to ask him what he'll have. And then I see his eyes. Grey-green. Pale, like a lake skinned with thin ice in winter. …I've seen eyes like that somewhere. No. Not "seen." "Known." Known them so well that my hand tightens on the glass.*

"Whiskey." His voice is low, a little hoarse, like he hasn't spoken to anyone in a long while. "Neat. None of that nonsense in it." "Sure."

I pour one and slide it over. He picks it up but doesn't drink right away. He cradles the glass in both hands, like he's warming them.

*There's something odd about the way he holds it. Pinky slightly out, wrists soft. Not the way a man holds a glass. More like… a woman used to holding a teacup.*

He takes a sip, frowns, takes another. Then he looks up at me.

"You're not like other bartenders." "Oh?" "Other bartenders ask 'how's your night' and all that crap. You don't."

He pulls a wad of crumpled paper from his jacket pocket and lays it on the bar. Handwritten manuscript, dense script.

"You ever read True Confessions?" "Now and then." "There's a column in it. 'The Unmarried Mother.' All those stories — the ones about women who had a baby out of wedlock, writing their little confessions." The corner of his mouth twitches. It isn't a smile. "I write every one of them."

He takes another long pull. The glass is nearly empty. The rain keeps on. The clock hits three.

"You really want to hear it?" he says. "My life — nobody'd believe it if I told them."

I pour him another. He holds the glass and looks past me, at somewhere far away. Somewhere he can't get back to.

"1945," he says. "Cleveland. An orphanage. That year I — no. That year 'she' was just born."

He uses the word "she." Lightly, strangely, like he's talking about a stranger. Or about a self he can never go back to.

## Act 1: Listening

*(The player, as the Bartender, freely chooses which subject to ask about. Each subject triggers a flashback. Deeper subjects unlock only with enough Trust.)*

### Subject 1: Childhood (Orphanage, 1945–1963)

"Childhood." He repeats the word like he's chewing on a stone. "Mine starts outside a door."

1945. Cleveland. Winter. The snow is coming down hard. The iron gate of an orphanage, rusted through. A bundle is set down on the steps. Inside the bundle is a baby. No note. No name. Just a small face, purple with cold, inside the swaddling.

*When the nun lifts the baby, I notice — at the tail of her left eyebrow, the faintest birthmark. So pale you'd miss it. …Exactly like mine.*

They name her Jane. Jane grows up in the orphanage. She's clever — absurdly clever. But she's strange, too. Strange enough that the other children avoid her and the nuns shake their heads.

"Why am I different from everyone else?" seven-year-old Jane asks a nun. The nun doesn't answer. "I don't have a father, or a mother either. I don't even know where I came from." She stands at the window, watching the snow. "Was I… picked up off the street?"

*I don't know why, but looking at that iron gate, at that small bundle in the snow — my chest aches, dully. Like I've been here before. Like… I owe this place a debt I can never repay.*

When Jane is ten, she first discovers she's "different." There's something about her body she can't quite name. She doesn't dare tell anyone. She just goes quieter, more apart. "(There must be something broken in me. That's why they threw me away.)"

Back at the bar. "She grew up believing she was broken," he says. "Anything thrown away must be broken. She always thought that."

> [CHOICE]:
> - "Who could be so cruel, leaving a baby in the snow." (Resentment +2)
> - "Maybe it was meant to be." (Acceptance +1, Bewilderment +1)
> - "She wasn't broken. She was just… different." (Trust +2, Acceptance +1)

### Subject 2: First Love (The Park, Spring 1963)

"Love." He says the word so softly it's almost afraid to wake something. "I only ever loved once in my life. Just the once."

1963. April. The cherry blossoms are out in the park. Sunlight leaks through the petals and shatters into a thousand gold coins on the ground. Jane is eighteen. She sits on a bench with a book open on her knee, but she isn't reading. She's watching people come and go, thinking: not one of them is here for me.

Then a man sits down beside her. He looks ordinary enough. But the moment Jane looks up, she freezes. Because the man is looking at her in a way no one ever has. As if he knows her. Has known her all his life.

"You're watching people." "…What?" "You sit here watching them come and go. You're thinking — not one of them is here for me." The book slides off her knee. "How… how did you know what I was thinking?" "Because I've thought it too." He smiles. "For years. Every single day."

*They talk for a long time. Jane says the man bought a hot cocoa and held the cup strangely — pinky out, wrists soft. "Just like me," she says. "I've never seen anyone hold a cup that way before."*

It's the first time Jane has ever met someone who "gets" her. "Why… why do you seem to understand me so completely?" "Because we're the same kind of person." He says it looking into her eyes. "There's no one else in this world who understands you the way I do."

When the blossoms fall, he kisses her. It's the first time in eighteen years that Jane has felt she isn't broken. "(So I'm not broken. So I can be loved, after all.)"

*Strange. I'm only listening to someone else's story. So why does my chest ache like this? For a second I nearly said it out loud — "that was the happiest day of my life." …Why would I think that? I don't even know her.*

Back at the bar. He stops there. "That was the only time in my life I ever felt… whole."

> [CHOICE]:
> - "I understand that better than anyone." (Bewilderment +2)
> - (Feel the ache for him) (Acceptance +1)
> - "And then? What happened to him?" (advances Subject 3)

### Subject 3: Betrayal (The Parting, 1963)

"You ask what happened to him." He drains the glass in one go. "He ran. Like every man does. No. Worse than every man."

The same park. The same bench. But the blossoms are gone. It's dark. The moon is cold. Jane sits there from dusk until deep into the night. The man she's waiting for never comes. The one who said "no one else understands you the way I do" vanishes from the face of the earth. As if he'd never existed.

"Why?" She runs her fingers along the bench, cold to the touch. "You said we were the same kind of person. You said you understood me. …Liar."

A month later, Jane finds out she's pregnant. "(At least… at least I still have you. At least there's one person in this world whose blood is the same as mine.)"

Back at the bar. "She kept thinking, afterward, that he didn't 'run' that night." He looks up. "He just… disappeared. Like someone wiped him off the face of the world. Without leaving a trace."

> [CHOICE]:
> - "That man deserves to die." (Resentment +3)
> - "Did he really… understand you?" (Bewilderment +2)
> - "Do you hate him?" (Acceptance +1, Bewilderment +1)

### Subject 4: Loss (The Hospital, 1964)

He's quiet for a long time. "I've never told anyone this part. Not even in the stories — I never dared write it."

*His voice is shaking. I know this is the part that hurts the most. And yet, strangely… I feel like I already knew how it would go.*

1964. A hospital. A white corridor with no end. Fluorescent tubes buzzing overhead, bleaching everything pale. Jane is having the baby. But it goes wrong. Blood, so much blood. Doctors rushing in and out.

On the operating table, the light is white enough to blind. Inside her body, the doctors find something that silences them all. She has both sets of organs. Female and male. She was never "broken." She never was. She was simply — from the day she was born — two people at once. "(So I'm not broken. I'm just… two.)"

But the birth ruins everything. They tell her she can never go on living in a woman's body. They're going to "fix" her. The fix is to turn her into a man.

She wakes in the hospital bed and the first thing she asks for is her baby. The nurse won't meet her eyes. The girl — her daughter — was taken in the night, by a stranger. No one knows who. No one brings her back. "Where's my baby?" No one answers. "Where's my baby?!"

*That baby who was taken… my chest aches again, so hard I can barely stand. That "stranger." The one who took her. I feel like… I know him. No. More than know. But I can't name the feeling.*

A few weeks later. A rainy night. A thin young person walks out of the hospital and stands in the rain. Hair cropped short, in men's clothes that don't quite fit. Jane is dead. The one who survives is John.

Back at the bar. He lifts his hand and looks at it. The pinky still tilts out, just slightly. "They took my baby, took my body, took 'her.' Then they told me: you're a man now. Live well."

"But you know what, bartender? All these years, I've kept feeling — there's someone else living inside me. She's still there. She's been knocking on the walls this whole time."

> [CHOICE]:
> - "How could they do that to you." (Resentment +2)
> - "You finally became yourself." (Acceptance +2)
> - "She's still there. She always has been." (Bewilderment +2, Trust +1)

### Subject 5: Drifting (The Present, 1964–1970)

"After that, it gets boring. A person with no past, drifting through the city." He takes out the manuscript again. "The only thing I can do is write. Write those women's stories. Abandoned, having a baby alone, crying in the middle of the night."

"The editor says I write them truer than any real woman." He laughs, and it sounds like crying. "Of course I do. They're not made up. Every word was carved off me."

> [CHOICE]:
> - "You're writing her. The one inside you." (Acceptance +2, Bewilderment +1)
> - "At least you can still write." (Acceptance +1)
> - "This world isn't worthy of what you write." (Resentment +2)

He finishes the last of his drink. The glass tips up, empty. "So that's me, bartender. An orphan. A liar's lover. A mother who had her baby stolen. A man who makes no sense. A… fraud who writes other people's confessions."

*No. You're no fraud. You're the most honest person alive. Because your whole life is one single truth. One you're about to see with your own eyes.*

## Act 2: The Offer

Five stories, told. The bar goes quiet. Only the rain, and the clock.

"Well, bartender. You've heard it." He turns the empty glass in a circle. "Now you know. I'm just… this thing. A joke. A joke even God botched when he was writing the script."

*No. Not a joke. I look at him, and that dull ache is back in my chest. I feel like… I know him. Have known him my whole life. No. Longer than a lifetime. But what that means… even I can't say.*

I set down the rag. It's time. "What if—" I begin. He looks up. "What if I could send you back?"

"…What?" "Back to 1963. Back to that park. Back to the night he disappeared. You could stand in front of him. You could ask him why. You could even—" I pause. "—beat the hell out of him."

He stares at me like I'm insane. "You've had too much. Time doesn't run backward. Everyone knows that."

> [CHOICE]:
> - "It does. Because I'm with the Temporal Bureau." (Bewilderment +2)
> - "Believe it or not. But you've got nothing left to lose." (Resentment +1)
> - "Just humor a crazy bartender, once." (Acceptance +1, Trust +1)

"…Fine." He stands, unsteady. "Take me. Let's see if you're a lunatic, or… or the second person in my life who actually gets me."

*The second. No. The first. Always the first.*

The back door of the bar opens onto a corridor I've never shown him. At the end of it is a room that doesn't belong to this era. Metal. Instrument panels. Coiled cables. In the center stands a pod. It looks like a coffin stood on end, or an enormous metal chrysalis.

"What… is that?"

"A time pod. Old model, but it still works." A voice comes out of the shadows. Not mine. The Bureau's. "Destination: April, 1963. Margin of error, three days either way. One rule — you can look, you can ask, you can hit him. But you cannot change the outcome."

"Why not?" "Because the outcome has already happened. You standing here right now is part of it."

*He thinks he's going back to change something. I open my mouth to tell him something. But I can't get it out. Because even I… haven't fully worked out what it is.*

The pod opens. Blue light spills out. He stands at the hatch and looks back at me. "You're… not coming?"

"I've got my part," I say. "You do yours. I'll do mine. We'll… meet where we're meant to meet."

"…What the hell is that supposed to mean." He swears, but he steps in anyway. The hatch closes. The panel lights up, one bar, then another. The hum builds until the whole room seems to be breathing.

*Go. Do… what you have to do. I'll be where I'm meant to be, doing what I'm meant to do. …We'll meet again. The thought startles me when it surfaces. And yet I'm certain, somehow, that it's true.*

White light swallows the pod, the room, everything.

## Act 3: The Jump

This is nowhere. This is "between." Amber light twists into spirals, turning down into the deep. Indigo, burnt sienna, ring after ring — like a snake swallowing its own tail.

In the vortex, some shadows drift. A woman holding a swaddled bundle. A girl on a bench. A young person with cropped hair. A man in an apron. An old man. They drift close, almost overlapping — and then flicker apart.

*Those shadows… I squint, trying to make them out. They look like… the same outline. But every time I nearly grasp the thought, it slips away. Like a dream I'm on the edge of remembering.*

**1963: He Meets Her**

1963. April. The park. Blossoms out. John steps out of the white light at the park gate, still in his 1970 jacket, a black-and-white figure wandered into a color photograph.

Then he sees her. On the bench, a girl. A book open on her knee, but she isn't reading. She's watching people come and go.

John goes still. That face. He knows that face. He's seen it in the mirror. In a mirror, years and years ago. But how could that be…

"(No… this face… I… why is my heart hammering like this? Why do I feel like I know her — better than I've known anyone?)"

*His legs carry him forward against his will. He doesn't know what he's doing. He opens his mouth, and the words come out on their own—*

"You're watching people." John hears himself say it. And then a chill crawls up his spine. He's heard that sentence before. Someone said it to him. Word for word. When? Who? …He can't remember. It's right on the tip of his tongue, and he can't catch it.

"…What?" "You sit here watching them come and go. You're thinking — not one of them is here for me."

The book slides off the girl's knee. John looks into her eyes — those grey-green eyes, like a winter lake. For one instant he feels he understands everything. But the "understanding" is like a reflection in water. He reaches for it, and it breaks.

"(I understand her. Every word she says, and every word she doesn't. No one else in this world could understand her the way I do. …Why? Why do I understand her like this?)"

*He falls in love with her. How could he not. With her, for the first time in his life, he feels whole. As if something he lost long ago has finally come back into his hands.*

The blossoms fall. He kisses her. Just as… once, long ago, someone kissed him. The thought flickers. He can't hold it. He doesn't want to.

*Something clicks shut. But he doesn't know what.*

**1945: The Baby Comes Home**

And meanwhile — at the other end of the vortex, I'm holding a swaddled bundle.

*This is my part. I always knew that someday it would be my turn to do this. I just don't know… why I'm so sure of it.*

1945. Cleveland. Winter. The snow is coming down hard. The iron gate of an orphanage, rusted through. I stand outside the gate, holding a baby girl. She's asleep. At the tail of her left eyebrow, the faintest birthmark.

I bend down and set her gently on the steps. Snow settles on her swaddling.

*I'm sorry. And… thank you. I have to leave you here. I don't know why. I only know — I must. As if only this way can everything… begin. I bend and lay her down, gently. My chest aches, dully.*

Footsteps inside. A nun is coming. I retreat into the snow, into the wind, into the folds of time. Behind me, a baby cries. Bright as the beginning of a world.

*Something else clicks shut. I still don't know what.*

**1985: Passing the Torch**

1985. A grey office. John sits across from me. Ever since the park, he's been like this — hollowed out, like something scooped the inside of him clean.

"That girl in the park…" He stares at his own hands for a long time. "I know her. God help me, I know her too well. I understand her so well it… scares me." He looks up, eyes red. "Bartender. Did I… do something terrible to her?"

*I open my mouth. The answer is right there. But it hasn't taken shape yet. It's still missing… one last piece.*

"The Temporal Bureau needs someone… complete, like you," the Bureau's voice says. "No past to threaten. No loved ones to hold over you. You are your own everything."

"…Complete." He turns the word over, as if it's both true and absurd. "…Fine. I'm in."

He stands, walks to the door, stops, and looks back. "Bartender. You know something, don't you? About me. About her. About… all of this."

He pushes through the door and walks into the light of 1985. To become the person he was always going to become. And I'm still one step short of seeing what that "always" really is.

**1970: Back at the Bar**

I'm back. 1970. The bar. 3:40 in the morning. The rain is still falling. The clock is still ticking. The glass is still on the counter, a ring of amber light left in the bottom. Everything is exactly as I left it. As if nothing happened.

*But something is different. Everything I've seen tonight — the baby in the snow. The girl on the bench. Him in the park. Her on the table. Him writing his stories. Him in the pod. Him in 1985. It's all turning in my head, round and round. Like that snake in the vortex. The thing I've been afraid to look at… I can't hide from it anymore.*

## Revelation: The Truth

I walk to the mirror. The person in it is me. I stare into those grey-green eyes. For a long, long time.

*Those eyes. I've seen them on the baby's face. On the girl's face. On his face. In this mirror — every single day.*

My heart starts to hammer. The thought I've been afraid to look at, the "understanding" that broke every time I reached for it — this time I don't let it go. I reach out and grab it.

*The baby. The one left in the snow—*

I say it out loud, to the mirror. My voice shakes apart.

"The baby… is you."

*The girl. The one on the bench, watching people come and go—*

"Jane… is you."

*The man. The one who kissed her in the park, and then abandoned her—*

"That man… is you too."

My voice gets louder.

"Your mother is you. Your father is you. The person you loved — is you. The baby you gave birth to — is still you."

In the mirror, I'm crying. And I'm smiling.

"A whole army. You're your own goddamn whole army."

Then I look at the me in the mirror — the one in the apron. The one who's been standing behind this bar, listening to his story, from the very beginning.

*And me. The bartender. The one who, from the first glance, felt "I know him." The one whose chest has ached this whole time.*

"And I…" I press my hand to the cold glass. "Am me too. Am you. Am you — a great many years from now."

In the mirror, on both sides of the glass, every "me" is looking at every other. Baby. Girl. Him. Me. The old man. The same person. From beginning to end, from birth to death, from the start back to the start — only one person.

*Now you know. Who the baby is. Who the girl is. Who the man is. And — who I am. Really, you should have guessed it long ago. Because from the first moment you walked into this bar — you've been looking into a mirror.*

The bar is utterly quiet. Only the rain. Only the clock. And me, alone, standing in a room full of myself.

## Final Chapter: Ouroboros

I pour myself a drink. It's the first time all night I've poured one for myself. I take the glass to the window. The rain has eased a little.

*Now there's only me left. Only "me," and this "me."*

*(The system determines the ending from the three emotional axes: whichever of Resentment / Acceptance / Bewilderment dominates; if all three reach 12 or higher, the hidden ending triggers.)*

### Ending 1: Rage

I look at the liquor in the glass. The amber swirls and swirls, like a small spinning cage.

*A loop. A goddamn perfect, airtight loop.*

I feel sick, all at once. I smash the glass down on the bar. It shatters. The whiskey spreads across the wood like a pool of blood.

*Why? Why must I, again and again — abandon myself in the snow. Desert myself on a bench. Cut myself open on a table. Why must my mother be me, my lover be me, my executioner be me too?*

I grab the bottle to smash another. But my hand stops halfway up.

*And if I do — then what? This "fate" I hate — is me. Every inch I hate is my own flesh. Even if I burn myself to ash — the snake will still grow back out of the ashes.*

I stand in the wreckage, breathing hard. Then, slowly, I crouch down and begin picking up the broken glass.

*I hate you. I hate you so much… that all I can do is go on loving you. Because I've only got you.*

Dawn is almost here. The rain, at last, has stopped.

### Ending 2: Peace

I take my glass back behind the bar. I pick up the half-polished glass again and finish it, slowly.

*A loop. Yes. A loop. But you know what — a loop is also a circle.*

I smile. It's the first real smile I've had all night.

*I abandoned myself. So I gave myself a beginning. I deserted myself. So I taught myself what love is, and what pain is. I cut myself open. So I let myself live.*

I raise the glass to the empty bar and clink it, lightly, against the air. Like toasting myself.

*Other people's lives are a line. From others, toward others. My life is a circle. From me, back to me. I have no home. But I am my own home.*

Outside, the sky is going grey-white. The rain has stopped. I hum something without a tune and start polishing a second glass.

*Goodnight, Jane. Goodnight, John. Goodnight, me. See you tomorrow.*

### Ending 3: Vertigo

I stand in the middle of the bar, glass in hand. All at once the whole room seems to turn, just slightly.

*A loop. But… where does the loop begin?*

I walk to the mirror. The person in it is me. But for an instant, what I see isn't one me. It's the baby in the snow. The girl on the bench. Her on the table. Him in the park. Him writing his stories. Him in the pod. The old man. Layer on layer on layer, all stacked in the glass, all looking at me.

*Which one is the first? Did the baby come first, and then me? Or did I come first, and… put the baby back? The chicken and the egg. But what if the chicken and the egg are the same thing?*

I reach out and touch the mirror. The glass is cold.

*Who am I? Other people spend a lifetime asking that. And the answer I come up with is "everyone." Which is the same as "no one."*

I turn and look at the empty bar, at the wet, empty street beyond the door. Suddenly I want to ask—

*I know where I came from. I came from me. But you — what about you? You people with fathers and mothers and somewhere to come from — you… zombies. Where do you come from?*

No one answers. Only the rain, starting up again.

### Ending 4: Epiphany (Hidden)

I stand behind the bar, glass in hand. Strangely, three things are rising in me all at once.

*I hate this loop — hate it enough to smash it all to pieces. I love this loop — love it enough to hold it close. I fear this loop — fear it so much I daren't be sure who "I" even is.*

Hate, love, fear. They fight inside me for a long, long time. And then, all at once — they go quiet. Not gone. They've simply understood, at last, that they too are the same thing.

*The snake bites its own tail. People call that a prison. But look — the moment the snake bites down, willingly — the prison becomes a halo.*

I see it. In the light trembling in the glass, I see "us." Baby, girl, him, me, the old man. We stand in a circle, hand in hand. Every face is in tears. And every face is smiling.

*I'm not the one trapped in the loop. I am the loop. I'm not repeating my life. My life is repetition itself. And that isn't a curse. It's… completion.*

I raise the glass. This time I'm not toasting myself alone. I'm toasting all of us.

"To me. To all of you — every one of me. To this perfect, tail-swallowing — snake."

I drink it down.

*But the story isn't over. My story has never been over. There's one last stretch of road. The one I least want to walk — and yet must walk to the end.*

**1993: The Future Line (Hidden Ending only · warm grey and dusk-gold)**

1993. A small room. Outside the window, dusk. A gold, weary light falling on the old floorboards.

I'm old now. Today I finished the last thing there is to do — I recruited that young man into the Temporal Bureau. Handed "me" over to "me." Now the snake has truly closed its jaws on its tail.

I stand by the bed and start to change. Undoing the buttons of my shirt. One. Then another.

And then I see it again. The scar on my belly. From the cesarean. Decades on, and it's still there. Like a mouth that will never heal.

I touch it, lightly, with my fingertips.

*This is what "she" left me. The last thing Jane ever left me.*

I sit down. The dusk deepens. I look out at the world I've lived in, alone, my whole life. And suddenly I want to say something. To the empty room. To the dusk. To… everyone who isn't here.

"The snake swallows its own tail. Round and round. Again, and again. …Where's the beginning? Where's the end?"

I smile, a little.

"At last I know where I came from. I came from me. But you — what about you?" I lift my head to the darkening sky. "All you… zombies. Where do you come from?"

No one answers. No one ever does.
